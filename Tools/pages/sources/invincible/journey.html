Beat 01

Wake In The Red

The opening begins with damaged context: mission-card fragments, an implied injury or recovery chamber, then Yasna alone on Regis III. The planet gets the first word, but the PC wrapper adds a second kind of fog.

Trying to screenshot or alt-tab did not pause cleanly, so some intro text advanced before it could be captured. That friction stays in the report instead of being hidden.

Review implication

Atmosphere starts high because the loneliness is immediate. Technical comfort gets an early watch mark because replay capture should not cost story context. The broad premise survived, but exact card loss matters for a PLATER evidence trail.

A: early strongT: wrapper watchR: repaired later
Beat 02

The Map Earns Trust

The next fragment stays foggy, but not uselessly foggy. Yasna appears separated, carrying gear through a desert-like area, then the game hands over a small inspection device and asks the player to read landmarks.

The M-key multicolored map landed well in the notes. That matters: The Invincible can be slow if it gives the player tools that make orientation feel diegetic.

Review implication

Readability and Tool Embodiment both move positive here. The question for later sessions is whether the map remains useful after onboarding or only feels good during the first reveal.

R: positiveTool embodiment +1Map trust
Beat 03

The Small Choice Matters

The first explicit choice is physical, not moral: gentle slope or risky slope. Daniel chose the gentle route after checking the space. That is not heavy branching, but it is clean decision traceability.

Then the sandstorm turns travel into another gap. Yasna blacks out and wakes much closer to the base; the ringed moon gives the planet a visual identity that sticks.

Review implication

Agency is low-stakes but real enough to log. Time Respect is ambiguous: the blackout may compress dead walking, or it may reduce control. That question stays open until the pattern repeats enough to judge.

Decision traceableTime respect watchRinged moon proof
Beat 04

The Camp Repairs The Timeline

The camp turns memory fog into objects: an unresponsive marching android, Dr. Krauta in deep stupor, and a radio/cable path that can reconnect Yasna to Novik/Base.

The comics/codex layer becomes more than flavor. It repairs context Daniel missed earlier: Dragonfly as a six-person research unit, Novik's rare-mineral injury, hibernation, the emergency near Regis III, surface lifelessness versus ocean life, and why the Invincible's arrival is suspicious.

Review implication

Readability improves because the game offers an in-world way to reconstruct missed context. Completion Shape remains a watch item until we know whether comics are automatic, route-based, or missable.

Codex repairKrauta identifiedCompletion watch
Beat 05

The Fish Should Not Know

The nearby tent adds a small water tank with fish-like life. The cutscene is long enough to become a Time Respect watch item, but its core observation is strong.

The fish fear robot probes even though they should not recognize them if they have never encountered such devices. The ocean-life clarification stops being lore and becomes a hypothesis engine.

Review implication

Scientific / Philosophical Density goes up here. The question is not merely whether there is life on Regis III; it is what that life has learned, perceived, inherited, or survived.

Hypothesis yieldFish/probe anomalyCutscene fatigue watch
Beat 06

The Camp Finally Acts Back

The camp segment becomes a solvable system: use Krauta's radio path, restore Base/Novik contact, examine Krauta, recover the mission log, follow Gorsky's trail, and identify the fried relay.

The important detail is the contradiction: ordinary sandstorm damage should not explain the relay. Scavenge a replacement, reboot the android, and the camp state actually changes.

Review implication

Loop improves because this is observation -> tool use -> log reading -> repair -> new state. AI Training Yield also rises: the run now has causal repair-chain reasoning, not only mood notes.
