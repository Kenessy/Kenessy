# 🧰 Rarity — Mundane → Keeper → Rare → Iconic → Relic

> [!tldr] 💎 Table TL;DR
> **Ladder (low → high):** **Mundane → Keeper → Rare → Iconic → Relic**  
> **What it measures:** how hard the thing is to replace **and** how the market reacts when it appears.  
> **Hard rule:** **Rarity = scarcity + market signal.**  
> **Do not mix axes:** *Scavenged / Clean / Rebuilt / Corrupted* are **tags** and can apply to **any** rarity.

---

## Concept — one word that stops arguments ^concept
Rarity is a **table-speed label**. It answers two questions fast:
1) **Replaceability:** “Can we get another one without changing our route?”  
2) **Attention:** “Does showing this draw buyers, thieves, priests, or auditors?”

Rarity is **not** a measure of morality, legality, or condition. Those are separate tags (below).

## Tier notes (quick, usable, non-numeric) ^tiers

### 1) Mundane 🪛
- **Availability:** common salvage, basic manufacture, routine vendor stock.
- **Expectation:** baseline function; no special permissions required.
- **Table tell:** nobody whispers when you put it on the counter.
-  Rusty red color. 

### 2) Keeper 🎒
- **Availability:** shows up regularly, but you still feel lucky.
- **Expectation:** **one** clear edge (reliability, fit, efficiency, a small utility trick) *without* rewriting the scene.
- **Table tell:** someone says “keep that” before anyone says “sell that.”
- Green color. 

### 3) Rare 💎
- **Availability:** usually gated by risk, access, or real trade leverage.
- **Expectation:** a meaningful feature or upgrade that players will plan around.
- **Table tell:** even experienced scavvers say “rare find” out loud.
- Blue color. 

### 4) Iconic 🏷️
- **Availability:** not shelf stock. You chase it, win it, steal it, inherit it.
- **Expectation:** multiple features **plus** an identity hook (maker-mark, signature behavior, rumor, ownership dispute).
- **Table tell:** it’s referred to as **“the”** thing, not **“a”** thing.
- Purple color. 

### 5) Relic 🗝️
- **Availability:** campaign-grade scarcity; functionally unique or uniquely consequential.
- **Expectation:** a new capability category, a rule-bend, or a setting-facing interaction—and always a cost, gate, or consequence.
- **Table tell:** the room changes when it appears: offers, threats, worship, contracts.
- Gold color.

## Tags (condition / provenance / heat) — recommended 🧷 ^tags
Tags describe *how* the item exists. They do **not** change the rarity rung by themselves.

**Common tags**
- **Scavenged** (assembled from parts), **Rebuilt**, **Jury‑Rigged**, **Overbuilt**
- **Clean** (stable / well-kept / untainted), **Pristine**
- **Marked** (maker stamp, faction brand), **Signed**
- **Hot** (wanted), **Quarantined**, **Cursed**, **Sanctified**

**Label format (on cards, lists, and crates):**  
**Rarity (Tag, Tag)** → *Rare (Clean, Marked)* / *Keeper (Scavenged)* / *Relic (Quarantined)*

> [!note] 🧠 Adversarial clarity rule
> A **Keeper** can be **Scavenged**. A **Relic** can be **Rebuilt**.  
> Tags are orthogonal; rarity stays the rung.

## Lore — why these five words exist here 🚂🕰️ ^lore

> [!quote] Read-aloud (one sentence)
> In the purple chronon haze after the Halt, the world became a museum—and the Express became the only auction house that still moved.

When the **Halt** tore time into pockets, “supply” stopped meaning factories and started meaning **reach**. Entire districts froze mid-breath: glass suspended, lightning hung like wire, doors locked forever behind deepstasis. Survivors learned fast that a find isn’t valuable because it’s shiny—it’s valuable because it’s **replaceable or not**, and because of what it **summons**: buyers, envy, prayers, violence.

Early on, crews fought over language as much as loot. Every market had its own claims: “mint,” “military,” “pre-Fall,” “holy,” “cursed.” None of it traveled well. Then the rail yards did what yards always do: they simplified.

Counters started chalking five words on crates and ledgers—the same boards where Leverage prices got posted—because those words ended negotiations faster than bullets.

- **Mundane** for the stuff that keeps you alive but doesn’t change your route.  
- **Keeper** for the step above: worth carrying, worth fixing, worth haggling over.  
- **Rare** for the things that make even hard people smile.  
- **Iconic** for the pieces with a face: a mark, a name, a story, a claimant.  
- **Relic** for the things that feel older than the living and heavier than metal—objects that make plans bend around them.

That ladder spread because it matched survivor speech. People actually say it in the field:
- “Mundane.”  
- “Keeper.”  
- “Rare find.”  
- “That one’s iconic.”  
- “Relic. Don’t flash it.”

### How higher-tier items appear (and why) ^origins
In AE, high rarity usually comes from one (or more) of these sources:

1) **Deepstasis access** — sealed rooms, stalled facilities, frozen vaults: the item wasn’t rare *then*, it’s rare **now** because the world is locked.  
2) **Survivor shaping** — years of repairs, swaps, and hard choices turn a tool into *the* tool; its reputation becomes part of its function (Iconic).  
3) **Anomaly pressure** — places where the laws fray (haze seams, surgefront scars, infernal bargains) create objects that don’t behave like objects (Relics).

## ✅ Acceptance checks (use these to prevent table drift) ^accept
- **Keeper is not “rare-lite.”** It means “worth keeping,” not “hard to find.”  
- **Rare is scarcity.** If you can buy it reliably every stop, it isn’t Rare.  
- **Iconic is recognition.** If it has a known mark/story/claimant, it’s Iconic even if the *effect* isn’t huge.  
- **Relic is consequence.** If it changes faction behavior, routes, or core assumptions, it’s a Relic even if it isn’t a weapon.  
- **Tags don’t promote tiers.** “Pristine” doesn’t automatically mean Rare; “Scavenged” doesn’t automatically mean Mundane.
