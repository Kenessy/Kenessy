
## Scene A2 — Obelisk, Clock & Postcards

> [!summary] **SCENE A2 — Obelisk, Clock & Postcards**  
> TYPE: Talk, Explore  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Safe, Timed  
> PURPOSE: Intro, Setup  
> TEACHING: Tutorial  
>  
> **When this runs:** After Scene A1, once the party has tested the Null a bit and is clearly looking for “what’s next.”  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Introduce the **Obelisk** as infrastructure of the Halt / Line.  
> - Show the **countdown** as a soft, reactive timer, not a trap.  
> - Seed the idea of **many lines/stations** (the postcard collage).  
> - Attach the **Silver Postcard** to one PC as a personal, persistent artifact.  
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Decide how they respond to an obvious “system object” in the Null.  
> - Explore the postcards and notice the **odd one out**.  
> - Someone chooses to keep the colour card (or you contrive that it ends up with someone).  
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Administrative, almost bureaucratic: this is how souls get sorted and boarded.  
> - The countdown is curious, not yet hostile; the system is watching but not angry.

---

### Block A2.1 — Obelisk Appears (Trigger & Read‑Aloud)

> [!question] **TRIGGER — When to bring this in**  
> Use this once:  
> - Everyone has acted/spoken in the wake‑up scene, **and**  
> - They’re clearly looking for “what’s next?” (asking about exits, scanning the space, or going quiet).

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The Obelisk & the Cards**  
> As you test the edges of this place, you realise there’s something in the middle of the platform you somehow kept overlooking.  
>  
> A tall obelisk of black stone stands there, mirror‑smooth and lightless. Near its top, a narrow window of pale numbers ticks gently backward, unhurried.  
>  
> Pasted to one face with ugly lumps of dried gum is a fan of postcards — most in grainy black‑and‑white station scenes, and one in bright, impossible colour near eye level.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — What’s here at a glance**  
> - **Obsidian Obelisk**  
>   - ~3–4 m tall, four‑sided, seamless; reflections smear instead of mirroring.  
>   - No seams, buttons, or runes at eye level; it feels *installed*, not natural.  
> - **Soft Countdown Window**  
>   - Small inset near the top, pale numerals counting **down**, not up.  
>   - Feels **slow and patient** — minutes, not seconds; present but not urgent.  
> - **Postcard Cluster**  
>   - 6–12 postcards stuck on with grey chewing gum.  
>   - Most are **black‑and‑white** views of stations/platforms (snow depot, jungle halt, seaside pier, desert siding, ruined yard, etc.).  
>   - One postcard is in **full colour**, slightly larger and fresher, near eye level, held by a fat wad of gum; it immediately draws the eye.

### Block A2.2 — Postcards & the Silver Card

> [!tip] **GM — How to use this block**  
> Use this once they approach the obelisk and start poking at the cards or asking about the images.

#### Looking over the cards

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Monochrome cards**  
> - Each shows a different station / platform scene in black‑and‑white: crowded, empty, ruined, pristine.  
> - If a PC peels one off:  
>   - It comes away easily, feels like an ordinary old postcard.  
>   - Kept on their person, it quickly feels dull and purely mundane — just a scrap of card.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Colour card (Silver Postcard)**  
> - Colours are **too saturated** for the Null; it looks almost alive.  
> - The image fits your AE tone: red‑lined train, storm‑lit viaduct, or a station the campaign will later visit.  
> - Each time they look away and back, small details have changed (lights, crowd positions, weather), but it’s clearly the same place.

#### Taking the colourful card

> [!tip] **GM — First touch vs choosing to keep**  
> - **Brief touch:**  
>   - Feels slightly **warm** and heavier than expected.  
>   - The gum resists, then relaxes; they *could* take it, but don’t have to yet.  
> - **Choosing to keep** it (studying it, tucking it away, “I take this one”) is what binds it.

> [!warning] **RULE — Binding the Silver Postcard (behind the screen)**  
> - The **first PC** who decides to **keep** the colour card becomes its bearer.  
> - Note `silver_postcard_bearer = <PC name>`.  
> - From then on, if the card is destroyed, discarded, or “lost,” it quietly **reappears** on or near that PC **between scenes**.  
> - Black‑and‑white cards are flavour only unless you deliberately promote one later.

> [!tip] **GM — What the bearer feels (pick 1–2)**  
> - A brief **vertigo**, like looking down an endless straight rail line.  
> - A clear single **tone** (station chime, violin note) only they hear.  
> - A flash of several **different stations** superimposed, then gone.  
> - A quiet, wordless sense of being **registered** or “on the list.”  
>  
> You can underline it with a line like:  
> *“Whatever this is, it feels like it belongs to you now — and the place seems satisfied with that.”*

> [!tip] **GM — If nobody takes it**  
> - Give them a fair window to choose.  
> - If absolutely no one engages, you may:  
>   - Have the colour card **unstick and fall** at someone’s feet, or  
>   - Later cut in with “you realise you’ve been holding that colour card this whole time.”  
> - Aim to have **someone** bound to it before Revival.

### Block A2.3 — Countdown Behaviour & Exit

> [!tip] **GM — How to use the clock (soft pressure only)**  
> - In this canto, the clock is **mood and framing**, not a fail‑timer.  
> - It should convey “**someone is watching the time**,” not “you’re punished for exploring.”

#### While they explore

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Background ticking**  
> - The numerals drift downward slowly; mention the ticking occasionally as ambient detail.  
> - If they ask “how much time do we have?”:  
>   - Answer loosely: “It’s going down, but not fast. You’ve got a bit of time.”

#### When they focus on it (or you’re ready to move on)

> [!tip] **GM — Speed‑up on attention / wrap cue**  
> - Once:  
>   - They’ve had a fair chance to explore the obelisk and cards, **and/or**  
>   - A PC explicitly studies the clock for more than a moment,  
> - Then describe:  
>   - The digits begin to flip a little **faster**, like the mechanism “notices” being watched.  
>   - The distant mechanical rhythm in the Null **locks to that faster beat**.

#### Trying to stop or hack the clock

> [!warning] **RULE — They can’t stop it in this canto**  
> - Scratching, covering, tapping, or talking to the display:  
>   - May cause a brief **glitch** (numerals smear, then re‑resolve),  
>   - But never halts, reverses, or meaningfully slows the countdown here.  
> - The point is to show that **time is supervised**, not to offer a solvable puzzle.

> [!success] **OUTCOME — Flags & next scene**  
> - Mark: `null_obelisk_seen = true`.  
> - Ensure `silver_postcard_bearer` is recorded once someone keeps the colour card.  
> - Table takeaway:  
>   - This pillar is a **board** for many stations/lines (monochrome cards).  
>   - One bright card has **chosen / been chosen by** a specific soul.  
>   - Time in the Null **responds to attention** and runs on rules outside their control.  
> - When the increased ticking is clear and the distant engine‑note rises into focus:  
>   - Describe twin red lines resolving in the dark and the approach of the Train.  
>   - Cut to **Scene A3 — Train Arrival & Boarding**.
