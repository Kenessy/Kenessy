## Scene B1 — Chapel of Second Chances

> [!summary] **SCENE B1 — Chapel of Second Chances**  
> TYPE: Talk, Explore  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Safe  
> PURPOSE: Intro, Setup  
> TEACHING: Reminder, Light  
>  
> **When this runs:** After **Scene A5 — Sundown Saloon & Lucifer’s Pact**, once out‑of‑game character creation is finished and the PCs step through the mirror on the bridge. This is the **first on‑Earth scene** in their new bodies, before they leave the ruined chapel.  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Bring the party **out of Hell and into the broken world**, in a single, contained space.  
> - Show that they now occupy **printed bodies** with **low HP and fresh Injuries** from the process.  
> - Let Lucifer deliver a **ground‑level brief**: the world’s state, the **Train** as their first major objective, and the existence of a nearby **side cache**.  
> - Introduce **PAVNN** as a low‑key, practical companion with local data.  
> - Quietly **return the Silver Postcard** so its bearer carries it into the chapter.  
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Wake up in their new bodies and **feel the cost** (HP + Injuries).  
> - Look around, poke at the **A‑Tech field camp** and its logs, and grab basic supplies.  
> - Talk to **Lucifer in the mirror** and to **PAVNN**; understand that the **Train station** is their first destination, with an optional cache nearby.  
> - Decide how they leave the crypt and in what **march order / mindset**.  
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Hungover, clinical, half‑sacred: a ruined chapel’s crypt turned into an improvised lab.  
> - The mood is **safe but not comforting** — fluorescent buzz over broken stone, faint thunder of the frozen apocalypse above.  

> [!danger] **RULE — System PARTIALLY ON (first bodies)**  
> - **HP, damage, skills, saves, spells, inventory, and Injuries are ON from here.**  
> - **Chaos, CDR, Harmony, Soul Debt, ASH, and other meta systems are still OFF** until they secure the Train.  
> - **Printing loop is not available yet.** If someone somehow dies before the Train, treat it as a **soft fail** — this canto assumes no lethal threats until after they reach the station.  
> - **Pact protection (PC vs PC):** Attacks between pact‑bound PCs **cannot deal HP damage or cause Injuries**. Swings can land, tempers can flare, but treat all direct harm as **cinematic only** until you deliberately lift this in a later chapter.  
> - **In this specific scene:**  
>   - No enemies, hazards, or damage sources appear **inside the crypt**.  
>   - All HP loss and Injuries here come from the **revival procedure** itself (below), not from new threats.

---

### Block B1.1 — Wake in the Broken Crypt (Trigger & Read‑Aloud)

> [!question] **TRIGGER — When to run this block**  
> - Run this **immediately after** players finish making characters and you cut back from the bridge‑mirror in A5.  
> - Treat this as the **in‑fiction moment** their pact signatures resolve and their chosen bodies wake.  
> - After this block, go to **B1.2** for the **Soul Grit wake check**, then use **B1.3–B1.6** in flexible order.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Coming back in stone and light**  
> Cold glass gives way to rough stone and stale air.  
>  
> You lie on narrow slabs in a low, half‑collapsed chamber — old brick and carved pillars choked with cables, lamps, and plastic crates. Harsh work‑lights buzz overhead; one heavy metal door waits at the far end, sealed with a blinking keypad.  
>  
> In one corner, a human‑sized frame of metal and canvas slumps in a loading chair, powered down. On the wall opposite, a floor‑to‑ceiling mirror hangs where a saint’s statue should be, its surface black and depthless.

> [!tip] **GM — Let them orient first**  
> - Give them a minute to **describe their new bodies** (link back to Null soul hints), check their gear, and trade a few in‑character lines.  
> - If they immediately try the door, it’s locked; the keypad flashes **red** at any random input.  
> - Once they’ve had **a little time** to react, run the **Soul Grit wake procedure** in B1.2.

### Block B1.2 — Soul Grit Wake Check (HP & Starting Injuries)

> [!tip] **GM — What this block is for**  
> This block turns “you wake up groggy” into a **single shared procedure** that:  
> - Sets their **starting HP** below max, and  
> - Hands out **their first Injuries** from printing.  
>  
> It uses the existing **Soul Grit** skill and **Injuries** rules; this is the **first time** those systems show up together, but in a **safe** context.

> [!abstract] **PROCEDURE — Revival hangover (once per PC)**  
> 1. **Explain in fiction:**  
>    - Their pact signatures have been **threaded into physical shells** Lucifer’s people staged here earlier.  
>    - The process was **rushed and ugly** — enough to walk and fight, not enough to feel good.  
> 2. Ask each player to roll **Soul Grit (SLG)** vs the **scene DC 13**.  
>    - Don’t say the number unless they ask; call out only **bands**: **CF, F, S, CS** per the Skills rules.  
> 3. For each PC, set **current HP** and assign **Injuries** based on their band:

**HP by band** *(cap at your max HP)*  

- **CF (≤ 8 total):** Set current HP to **1**.  
- **F (9–12):** Set current HP to **3**.  
- **S (13–17):** Set current HP to **5**.  
- **CS (≥ 18):** Set current HP to **7**.  

**Starting Injuries** *(revival scars)*  

- **All bands:** Each PC gains **1 Minor Injury** from the print.  
  - GM offers **two plausible lanes** (often **RES**, **SLG**, or a key physical skill they leaned on in life); the player picks one.  
- **On F or CF:** They gain **a second Minor** on a **different** eligible lane (GM offers two more; player picks).  
- **On S or CS:** They stay at **just the 1 Minor**.  

Apply normal **Injury assignment rules** (no stacked Minors on a lane; merge to Majors later, cap at 3 Majors total).

> [!tip] **GM — How to describe it**  
> - On **CF/F:** emphasise shaking hands, stabbing headaches, muscles that feel like they’ve been rebuilt wrong.  
> - On **S/CS:** they still feel off — stiff joints, faint tremor — but clearly better off than some of their companions.  
> - Let players **colour what the scars mean** for them; you just attach them to lanes.

### Block B1.3 — Exploring the Broken Crypt

> [!tip] **GM — Use this once they start looking around**  
> This block gives you **quick answers** when they poke at the environment, plus a small amount of **safe loot and lore**.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — At a glance**  
> - **Layout**  
>   - A low, rectangular crypt chamber under the ruined chapel.  
>   - 3–5 stone slabs with cable harnesses (their “beds”).  
>   - One **reinforced door with keypad** leading up toward the chapel; no other exits.  
> - **Lighting & sound**  
>   - Harsh portable floods on tripods; cables snake into the dark ceiling.  
>   - Faint distant rumbles like far‑off artillery or collapsing buildings — the frozen apocalypse above, still echoing.  
> - **A‑Tech camp**  
>   - Plastic crates, folding tables, a couple of rugged laptops or consoles, field med‑kits, and sealed ammo boxes **they can’t open yet**.  
>   - Most screens show frozen status windows and error logs about “Timeline Integrity” and “Subject Threads”.

#### Searching for gear

> [!abstract] **LOOT — What they can reasonably find here**  
> - **Medical:**  
>   - 2–4 **basic healing items** appropriate to your system (potions, injectable stims, bandage kits; enough to matter but not solve everything).  
> - **Supplies & tools:**  
>   - A few **generic tools** (flashlight, multitool, tape, chalk, markers) and 1–2 flavourful but minor trinkets.  
> - **Weapons & armour:**  
>   - Assume their **starting loadouts** mostly come from character creation.  
>   - You may seed **1–2 extra low‑tier items** if someone built very “poor” on gear, but keep this room as **light prep**, not a full armoury.

#### Logs, laptops, and datapads

> [!abstract] **LORE — What the files say**  
> - Most logs are dry **A‑Tech field reports** about “body acquisition,” “signal drift,” and “Lucifer contact: cleared.”  
> - A few lines you can drop in as they skim:  
>   - “—subject threads stable within tolerance after pact signature—”  
>   - “—time‑halt pockets spreading out from ground‑zero; recommend immediate asset extraction—”  
>   - “—‘train’ project nearly ready; awaiting final boiler element—”  
> - If they dig harder, they can find **one or two named NPCs or codenames** you plan to use later; keep it light, just enough to reward curiosity.

> [!tip] **GM — Keep the door shut**  
> - If they try to **force the door**, it’s beyond what they can reasonably brute‑force at level 1; you can say:  
>   - “The lock here is more about codes than muscle — you’d need the right sequence, not a crowbar.”  
> - The keypad itself is **inert** until Lucifer gives them a working code in B1.4.

### Block B1.4 — Lucifer in the Mirror (Briefing)

> [!tip] **GM — When to bring Lucifer in**  
> - Wait until:  
>   - They’ve rolled the **Soul Grit wake check**,  
>   - Had a short window to look around / grab a few things, **and**  
>   - Start talking about “what now?” or poking at the door/mirror.  
> - Then the mirror “catches” the light and Lucifer arrives.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — “Gather around me.”**  
> The black mirror ripples once, like something pushed up from the other side. A man in a dark suit and undone collar stands there, elbows on a bar that doesn’t exist in this room.  
>  
> His eyes track each of you, sharp and amused.  
> “Gather around me,” he says lightly. “Since you went to all the trouble of surviving.”  

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Pacts, bodies, and time**  
> He straightens his tie with one finger. “The pact is live. So are you. My people dragged your threads out of the Null and into these very expensive shells.”  
>  
> “Consider this the end of the hand‑holding. Once you step through that door, you’re on the clock. I can’t hop from mirror to mirror babysitting you — I am, tragically, very busy keeping a dying timeline from collapsing.”  

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The state of the world & the Train**  
> “You remember the headlines. Wars, little plagues, big egos. Eventually someone pushed the wrong stack of buttons and lit the sky.” He gestures vaguely upward. “Your upstairs neighbour responded with angels and trumpets. Double exposure: man‑made suicide, divine eviction.”  
>  
> “I did what I could. I stepped out onto the film and pressed pause. It didn’t stop cleanly. Some places are frozen mid‑scream; others judder forward in fits and starts. That strain you feel in your bones? That’s the world trying to finish dying.”  

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The Apocalypse Express gift**  
> “To move in that mess, you need something special. Cars don’t behave well when time is arguing with itself.” His mouth quirks. “So I invested. Bought a little local steam relic, tore out its insides, and rebuilt it around one of my own tricks.”  
>  
> “There’s a fragment of hellfire burning in its boiler now. Just a coal. Enough to let the train cut through the halt and ride the cracks between seconds. It was… not inexpensive.”  

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The first job**  
> “Your first step is simple on paper.” He taps a fingertip against the mirror glass; it pings like metal. “Get to the station. Take my train.”  
>  
> “The station is close enough to walk. There’s also a smaller little… opportunity nearby — an old cache some of my contractors used. If you feel under‑dressed, you can stop there first. You’ll find rumours, maps, maybe a gun that doesn’t fall apart.”  

> [!tip] **GM — Let them ask a few questions**  
> - Give them **space to interrogate him**. Notes for Lucifer’s answers:  
>   - He **never lies**, but he **dodges and trims**; he will not admit that the “save” may be a **full rewrite** of the world.  
>   - On “why help?” he leans on **habit and aesthetics**: “I’ve grown attached. It would be a shame to waste all this drama.”  
>   - On “what happens if we refuse?” he calmly points out that leaving the chapel unarmed in a dying world is **not a survival plan**.  
> - If they bog down, he glances at an unseen watch and starts steering toward PAVNN and the exit.

### Block B1.5 — PAVNN Wakes & Marching Orders

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Waking PAVNN**  
> Lucifer’s gaze slides off you to the slumped frame in the corner. “Right. Almost forgot.”  
>  
> He snaps his fingers. The android’s chest panel glows once, then again, brighter. Servos whine softly as it unfolds from the chair, eyes coming up with a faint amber ring.  

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — PAVNN in this canto**  
> - **Role:** cargo‑loader turned liaison. Low‑key personality, designed to work, not to lead.  
> - **Capabilities:**  
>   - Can **carry gear**, manage simple tools, and handle basic repairs or doors once you author those scenes.  
>   - Not built for frontline combat; in fights it tends to **hunker down** or **follow simple orders**.  
> - **Information:**  
>   - Has basic **local maps** and **mission notes** once it downloads from the datapads here.  
>   - Knows there is a **station** and a **nearby cache** and can point roughly where, but not full details.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Lucifer’s orders to PAVNN**  
> “PAVNN,” Lucifer says, as if addressing a clerk. “Escort these souls to the Train. Make sure they reach me with the package in hand.”  
>  
> The android’s voice is flat but clear. “Acknowledged. Objective: support contracted souls. Destination: Apocalypse Express, then final rendezvous.” It glances at you with the mild curiosity of a machine seeing new cargo.

> [!tip] **GM — Let PAVNN ‘read’ the room**  
> - Have PAVNN slot one of the **datashards/laptops** they found.  
> - In a few lines, you can have it summarise:  
>   - The **station** distance / direction in simple terms.  
>   - The existence of a **secondary site** (cache, safehouse, or ruins) the PCs can choose to hit first.  
> - Keep PAVNN as a **supporting voice**, not a second GM: short, factual answers, no big speeches.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Door code & exit line**  
> Lucifer looks back to you. “You have your bodies. You have your first map. And you have a very expensive train waiting to be stolen on your behalf.”  
>  
> He recites a short string of numbers and symbols; the keypad by the door blinks green. “That opens this door and this door only. From here, you walk yourselves. We’ll talk again when you find the Sundown Saloon.”

### Block B1.6 — Silver Postcard & Exit

> [!tip] **GM — When to trigger this**  
> - Fire this beat **as they’re about to leave** the crypt:  
>   - After the door code is entered, **before** they physically step through, or  
>   - As a last little sting right after Lucifer’s image fades.

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Silver Postcard reasserts**  
> - The **bearer** from earlier feels a brief, sharp **heat** at their pocket, belt, or pack.  
> - When they check, the **Silver Postcard** is simply **there again**, as if it never left.  
>   - The image on the front still shows whatever you established before (rails, station, or train scene).  
>   - The back still bears its **first verse**; no new lines appear here.  
> - If the bearer truly forgot about it, this is a gentle reminder that **some things are glued to their thread**, not their inventory.

> [!success] **OUTCOME — Flags & next scene**  
> - All PCs are now **awake in bodies**, with **HP set** by the Soul Grit check and **starting Injuries assigned**.  
> - **PAVNN** is active and attached to the party as a **support NPC**.  
> - PCs know, at minimum:  
>   - The world is in a **paused double apocalypse**.  
>   - Their **first major goal** is to reach the **station** and seize the **Apocalypse Express**,  
>   - There is a **nearby cache/side site** they can choose to hit first.  
> - The **Silver Postcard** is back in the bearer’s possession as they step into the wider world.  
> - Suggested flags to set:  
>   - `chapel_revived = true`  
>   - `pavnn_online = true`  
>   - `ae_station_briefing_heard = true`  
>   - `silver_postcard_reasserted = true`  
> - When you are ready, cut to **Scene B2 — Broken Chapel Yard** (or your chosen next scene) as they emerge into the ruined church above.
