(Stanza)## Scene A3 — Train Arrival & Boarding

> [!summary] **SCENE A3 — Train Arrival & Boarding**  
> TYPE: Talk, Explore  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Safe  
> PURPOSE: Intro, Setup  
> TEACHING: Tutorial  
>  
> **When this runs:** After Scene A2 (Obelisk & Postcards) once the countdown has clearly sped up and “hits zero.”  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Manifest the **twin red rails** and the **Apocalypse Express** as a vast, half‑living machine.  
> - Introduce the **Android Herald** who announces boarding and will not answer questions.  
> - Let players stall or resist, then learn that **boarding is inevitable** if they want play to continue.  
> - Move all PCs into a **sealed interior carriage**; doors closed; train departing.  
> - Give the **Silver Postcard** its first signature reaction (heat + 4‑line poem about this train/fate).  
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Decide how they feel and behave as the train arrives (awe, fear, bravado, reluctance).  
> - Choose whether to board promptly, drag their feet, or protest (knowing the scene will wait on them).  
> - If holding the Silver Postcard, decide whether to look at it when it flares.  
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Awe first: two red lines tear reality open and a monstrous train slides in.  
> - Calm inevitability second: a scheduled service, not a chase; the system arrived exactly when it meant to.

> [!danger] **RULE — System still OFF (Null rules persist)**  
> - No HP, damage, skills, saves, spells, Chaos, CDR, Harmony, currencies, or Injuries.  
> - The Android, rails, and train **cannot be harmed**; attempts behave like other Null violence (phasing, rewind, no marks).  
> - Nothing in this scene spends resources or kills them “again.”  
> - Any dice you roll are **for colour only**, never success vs failure.

### Block A3.1 — Trigger & Arrival

> [!question] **TRIGGER — When to bring the train in**  
> Use this once:  
> - The Obelisk countdown in A2 has visibly sped up and you’ve described the last flicker of light as it “hits zero.”  
> - The table is clearly ready for “what happens now?”

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Rails & Train**  
> The little window of ticking numbers on the obelisk flares once, then goes dark.  
>  
> Around the platform’s rim, the thin red glow swells and pulls itself up into two solid bars — rails growing out of the stone like hot metal vines. Heat and the smell of iron rise as they harden and settle.  
>  
> From the dark beyond, something colossal slides into view along those brand‑new tracks: a train, all iron ribs and breathing vents, half machine and half something that remembers being alive. Cars roll past in a blur until one set of doors glides to a perfect stop in front of you.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — What’s here now (outside)**  
> - **Twin red rails**  
>   - Rise straight out of the platform and run off into the dark; no visible origin or end.  
>   - Glow like banked coals; you feel heat on your face but it never burns.  
> - **Apocalypse Express (exterior)**  
>   - Long enough to feel wrong; the visible cars seem to repeat or slide as you look.  
>   - Surfaces flex subtly, as if metal is stretched over something that breathes.  
>   - Windows show hints of corridors and carriages, but details smear when stared at.  
> - **Boarding door**  
>   - One wide door aligns exactly with the platform edge and sighs open.  
>   - Dim, warmer light and the outline of a carriage interior wait beyond.  
> - **Obelisk**  
>   - Countdown window is dark; monochrome postcards look flat and lifeless.  
>   - The stone now feels like powered‑down equipment: present, but inactive.

### Block A3.2 — The Android Herald

> [!tip] **GM — Introducing the Herald**  
> - As soon as the boarding door is open, bring the Android out.  
> - It makes a **clear announcement**, then mostly just waits and repeats.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Android Herald**  
> - **Look**  
>   - Tall, humanoid, in a dark conductor’s coat and cap.  
>   - Face like smooth porcelain, with only the suggestion of features.  
>   - Eyes are steady pale points; no pupils.  
> - **Behaviour**  
>   - Movements are precise and a half‑second late, like playback catching up.  
>   - Steps down from the train, turns to face the souls, and stands just to one side of the open door.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Announcement (sample)**  
> “Apocalypse Express, Red Line service.  
>  
> All manifested souls are expected to board.  
>  
> Departure will commence when boarding is complete.”

> [!warning] **RULE — How the Herald responds**  
> - It does **not** answer real questions. For anything they ask, it either:  
>   - Repeats or lightly varies the script, e.g.:  
>     - “You are expected.”  
>     - “Departure follows boarding.”  
>     - “Questions may be addressed inside the carriage.”  
>   - Or falls silent and simply looks at them, then at the open door.  
> - It cannot be pushed, grappled, or harmed. All such attempts behave like other Null violence: phasing, brief visual glitch, then everything snaps back.

### Block A3.3 — Boarding, Stalling, Refusing

> [!tip] **GM — Soft choice, fixed outcome**  
> - Players can posture, stall, or protest as much as they like.  
> - The fiction always resolves to: **all PCs end up inside the carriage, doors closed, train leaving**.  
> - Make that clear out of character if the table seems uncertain.

#### Boarding promptly

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Stepping aboard**  
> - Crossing the threshold feels like walking through a thin film of **pressure and warmth**.  
> - On the far side is a long, dim **carriage interior**:  
>   - Benches or seats along both sides, metal ribs curving overhead.  
>   - Narrow windows showing the red rails and dark beyond.  
> - The Android steps aside to leave the path clear and, if you like, follows the last PC in.

#### Stalling on the platform

**Player move:** hangs back, argues, keeps asking questions, refuses “for now”.

> [!tip] **GM — Handling stallers**  
> - Train and Herald simply **wait**. Nothing moves, nothing escalates.  
> - The Herald may repeat lines like:  
>   - “All manifested souls are expected to board.”  
>   - “Departure will commence when boarding is complete.”  
> - PCs who already boarded:  
>   - See a still interior and feel time on “hold.”  
>   - Can shout to the holdout if you want; voices carry normally.

#### Trying to refuse / run / jump / attack

**Player move:** “I refuse to board,” runs around the platform, jumps off, dives under the train, attacks the Herald or the cars, etc.

> [!warning] **RULE — Null physics still apply**  
> - **Running away:**  
>   - The platform loops; no matter which way they run, they eventually find themselves near the boarding door again.  
> - **Jumping off / under the train:**  
>   - They get a beat of falling or impact — then reality skips and they’re simply back by the door, upright and unharmed.  
> - **Attacking Herald / train / rails:**  
>   - Blows and spells pass through or land with no effect; at most, the scene stutters and resets.  
> - After 1–2 good attempts, you can gently say out of character:  
>   - “You can’t break this place’s rules from here. If you want the story to continue, at some point you have to step onto the train.”

> [!tip] **GM — Let social pressure do work**  
> - Let other PCs shout, cajole, or drag the holdout in fiction.  
> - The Herald never threatens them; it just waits with perfect patience.

### Block A3.4 — Silver Postcard Reaction

> [!tip] **GM — When to trigger this**  
> - Trigger once the train is fully stopped and the announcement has landed.  
> - Do this whether the bearer is on the platform or has already stepped inside.

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Heat & verse**  
> - The bearer feels a sudden, focused **heat** where the Silver Postcard rests (pocket, belt, hand).  
>   - Hot enough to demand attention, never enough to injure.  
> - If they check it:  
>   - The previous image has shifted to echo the scene outside: rails, train, or station.  
>   - On the red‑tinted back, a new **four‑line poem** has appeared.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The first poem** *(example; you can replace)*  
> The card’s surface is hot as you turn it over. Against a deep, ember‑red field, four lines of black text wait:  
>  
> *Drawn on rails that cut the dark,*  
> *Fate arrives in iron and spark;*  
> *Every soul that walks this line*  
> *Takes the bargain, signs in time.*  

> [!warning] **RULE — What this actually does**  
> - Only the **bearer** feels the heat and sees the change first; others can read the poem if it’s shown.  
> - In Canto I, this has **no mechanical effect**: it is pure foreshadowing.  
> - Note that the postcard has shown **Verse #1** (`silver_postcard_verse_1 = true`) for later chapters.

### Block A3.5 — Interior Carriage & Exit

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Inside the first carriage**  
> - **Layout**  
>   - One long aisle, benches or seats along both sides.  
>   - Curved metal ribs overhead with faint red light strips.  
> - **Windows**  
>   - Narrow and dark; staring out shows the rails and void sliding away in streaks as the train moves.  
> - **Doors**  
>   - The external door behind them seals with a solid, airless click once the last PC boards.  
>   - Any internal doors forward or back are closed; handles feel fixed and unresponsive if tested.

> [!tip] **GM — How to close the scene**  
> - Give them a beat to choose seats, stand, or react in character.  
> - Let them feel that they are now **inside something else’s system**, not the Null.  
> - No staff interact here yet; the Herald can stand at one end or step through an internal door and vanish between scenes.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Departure**  
> The door seals behind you with a soft, final thump.  
>  
> For a moment, everything is still. Then a low hum rises under your feet and the carriage shivers. Outside the narrow windows, the red rails and black void smear into motion as the train begins to move.
