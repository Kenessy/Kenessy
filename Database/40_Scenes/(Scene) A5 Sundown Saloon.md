## Scene A5 — Sundown Saloon & Lucifer’s Pact

> [!summary] **SCENE A5 — Sundown Saloon & Lucifer’s Pact**  
> TYPE: Talk  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Safe  
> PURPOSE: Climax, Setup  
> TEACHING: None  
>  
> **When this runs:** Immediately after Scene A4, once the Android has escorted the PCs toward the **Sundown Saloon** and the train is crossing an impossible bridge through Hell. This is the **last pre‑Revival scene** before PCs wake in new bodies.  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Reveal that this is **Hell**, and outline the **dual apocalypse** on Earth.  
> - Explain Lucifer’s **time‑stop “pause”** and why he needs agents, not himself, on the surface.  
> - Offer the **Orb mission**: find the object and bring it to Lucifer at the end of the world.  
> - Have every continuing PC **sign Lucifer’s pact** and step toward Revival.  
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Decide how they react to Lucifer’s pitch and to learning they are in Hell.  
> - Ask questions, test his logic, and decide to **sign or refuse** (refusal = that PC stays in Hell, see GM notes).  
> - Give small glimpses of what they want out of a second life (for Lucifer’s future banter / your psych ledger).  
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Old‑money calm at the edge of catastrophe; dry humour, no shouting.  
> - Lucifer is polite, prepared, and clearly dangerous; his cruelty hides under impeccable logic.  
> - The world is on pause; this scene is the **sales pitch at the brink**.

> [!danger] **RULE — System still OFF (pre‑Revival)**  
> - As in A1–A4, **no HP, damage, skills, saves, spells, Chaos, CDR, Harmony, currencies, or Injuries** apply yet.  
> - Lucifer, the train, the bridge, and Hell **cannot be harmed**; Null/Train physics still protect the scene.  
> - Signing the pact is **purely fiction** for now: no stats change, no mechanics flip here.  
> - System turns **ON** only once you move into the **Revival — Lucifer & Quantum Print** procedure after this scene .

### Block A5.1 — Entering the Sundown Saloon

> [!question] **TRIGGER — When to run this scene**  
> - After the Android in A4 says: “Please proceed to the Sundown Saloon for final processing.”  
> - Run once the PCs are guided into the **bar‑car** and the doors close behind them.  
> - Outside, the train is crossing an **impossibly long bridge** suspended over Hell.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The Saloon**  
> Warm amber light spills across polished wood and brass as you step through. This carriage is an old western lounge upgraded for eternity: leather booths, a long mirrored bar, and wide windows looking out over an endless bridge hanging above a burning chasm.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Sundown Saloon at a glance**  
> - **Car style**  
>   - Old but **premium** western bar car: polished wood, brass rail, mirror‑backed bar.  
>   - Updated with subtle modern details: perfect lighting, no dust, everything exactly placed.  
> - **Windows & outside**  
>   - One side looks out across an **impossible bridge** of stone and steel running into crimson haze.  
>   - Far below: refinery lights, rivers of fire, distant structures — Hell as an endless industrial city.  
> - **Interior mood**  
>   - Completely **empty** apart from the PCs and Lucifer: this is a **VIP lounge for souls**.  
>   - Soft hum underfoot; faint vibration of the train on the rails; low bar‑jazz that never quite resolves.  
> - **Bridge logic**  
>   - Lucifer will later lead them **out onto this bridge** and toward the Mirror gate to Revival.  
>   - For now, the bridge is only visible, not physically reachable.

> [!tip] **GM — Where’s the Android?**  
> - The Android **does not** stay. As soon as the PCs enter:  
>   - He gives a short nod and closes the door behind them.  
>   - He won’t return in this scene; it’s just the PCs and Lucifer.

### Block A5.2 — Lucifer at the Bar (Entrance & Profile)

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — Lucifer at a glance**  
> - **Look**  
>   - Tall, immaculate, late‑30s / early‑40s vibe; dark waistcoat, crisp shirt, old‑fashioned tie.  
>   - Movements are precise and unhurried; he always seems **half a beat ahead** of the room.  
>   - Eyes catch the light strangely; not glowing, but the pupils never quite resolve.  
> - **Manner**  
>   - Speaks quietly; never raises his voice or loses his temper.  
>   - Humour is dry and cutting, but rarely overtly cruel; he prefers **rationale over rage**.  
>   - Hates humanity as a whole, but clearly **enjoys individuals** as toys, projects, or favourites.  
> - **Truth constraints**  
>   - **He cannot lie.** He will, however, omit, reframe, and answer sideways.  
>   - If you’re unsure at the table, favour: **true, but incomplete** over “twisty half‑truth.”  
> - **Knowledge of the PCs**  
>   - He has **blurred impressions** of their deaths and lives, not crisp detail.  
>   - When pressed, answer in broad strokes: mood, cause (“violence,” “sickness,” “sacrifice”), not full biography.  
>   - Use this to hint at backstory, not to overwrite what players want.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Lucifer greets them**  
> Behind the bar, a man in a dark waistcoat polishes a spotless glass, as if you interrupted him in his own quiet evening; his eyes catch the firelight oddly when he looks up. He smiles and says, “At last. Welcome to my carriage.”

> [!tip] **GM — When they ask “who are you?”**  
> - Keep it **simple and classic**:  
>   - “Lucifer will do.”  
>   - “The fallen one, if you like titles.”  
>   - “Owner of the rails you’re standing on.”  
> - He does **not** monologue his whole history; he assumes they’ve heard of him.

### Block A5.3 — The Pitch in Beats (Hell, Earth, and the Pause)

> [!tip] **GM — How to use this block**  
> - This is the big exposition, but **don’t drop it all at once**.  
> - Use each **Beat** as a 20–40 second chunk, then stop to let players react or ask questions.  
> - Take notes for your **Lucifer psych ledger** as they respond (Mercy / Efficiency / Ruthlessness / Mark) .

#### Beat 1 — The view below (anchoring Hell)

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Hell as a city**  
> He saunters to the nearest window and tips his glass toward the view. Far below, an industrial hellscape glows—rivers of fire, black towers, and a lattice of bridges fading into smoke—as he says lightly, “First floor. Consequences.”

> [!tip] **GM — Hook one soul to the view**  
> - Ask: “Down in that grid of fire, one landmark looks uncomfortably familiar to someone — a building, a street, or a place tied to your death. Who recognises something, and what is it?”  
> - Let one PC briefly describe it; Lucifer listens, amused, but doesn’t comment much.

#### Beat 2 — Dual apocalypse (what happened to Earth)

> [!quote] **READ ALoud — Two endings at once**  
> He sets the glass down and counts on his fingers. “You died,” he says, “because your species finally pulled the trigger — war, famine, little pet disasters, choose one — and lit the sky, and then my upstairs neighbour flipped to the ending and sent angels down to evict everyone.”

> [!tip] **GM — Tone for questions here**  
> - If they ask **“Is this really Hell?”**:  
>   - “You’re below where you started and above where you could be. Call it Hell, if labels help.”  
> - If they ask **“Are the angels the good guys?”**:  
>   - “They’re efficient. Good is a marketing term.”

#### Beat 3 — The time‑stop (Lucifer’s leverage)

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Holding the frame**  
> He taps a fingernail against the bar; the chime hangs in the air instead of fading, a thin note in your teeth. “That’s your timeline,” he says, closing his hand on nothing, “and I am the one holding the pause between one second and the next.”

> [!tip] **GM — Memory glitch prompt**  
> - Ask each player for **one glitched image** from the moment before they died:  
>   - “Colour wrong, sound looping, someone’s face pixelated — give me one corrupted frame from your last second.”  
> - Keep responses short; this is about **emotion + weirdness**, not full flashbacks.

#### Beat 4 — The Orb analogue (what he needs)

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Hardware, not prophecy**  
> He pushes his hand casually into the solid bar as if it were water and draws out a fist‑sized sphere of matte black metal veined with faint gold. “Its twin sits in a vault at the end of the world,” he says, “and if I go in person, your reality tears.”

> [!tip] **GM — Optional prop beat**  
> - If you like props, drop a **heavy object** (big die, paperweight) on the table.  
> - Lucifer: “Not magic. Architecture. Someone pick it up.”  
> - Whoever does can describe an instinctive **pull** or unease; don’t over‑explain it yet.

#### Beat 5 — The bargain (job & reward)

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The job**  
> He lets the sphere sink back into the bar like it was never there. “You go back,” he says, “you locate the orb, and you deliver it to me where the world comes apart, so I can turn this pause into something like a reset instead of an ending.”

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The reward & the threat**  
> “In exchange,” he continues, “I stitch you back into the story instead of leaving you on the cutting‑room floor — new bodies, a head start, the kind of lives most mortals would sell their souls for: power, comfort, unfinished business, whatever fits. Or you can decline, stay here, and watch the smoke while it lasts.”

> [!tip] **GM — Get their priorities on record**  
> - Ask each player:  
>   - “If he’s telling the truth, what one thing in that offer matters most to your soul — power, safety, fixing something, someone else?”  
> - Write **one or two words per PC** on your Lucifer psych ledger; this is gold for later scenes.

> [!tip] **GM — “Why are you helping humanity?”**  
> - Lucifer never says “this is my redemption arc,” but he also doesn’t lie. Favourites:  
>   - “I got used to you. It would be a terrible waste.”  
>   - “Am I not gracious? Even now, I’m willing to save the species trying hardest to die.”  
> - If they push: he shrugs, lets the silence do the work, then changes the subject back to the deal.

### Block A5.4 — Contracts on Skin & the Golden Tickets

> [!tip] **GM — This is where the tickets pay off**  
> - Players already found **Golden Tickets** in A4.  
> - Here, those tickets **become the visible pact**: no new items, just a transformation.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Tickets turn into contracts**  
> As his gaze lingers on your pockets, each golden ticket warms and flips in your hand of its own accord. The back is no longer blank cardstock but thin, pale parchment scored with shifting black and red script, as if the writing were trying out different clauses while you watch.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Lucifer’s aside**  
> “Strictly speaking it’s cured human skin,” he notes almost absently, “but don’t be sentimental — the originals were very proud of the arrangement.” Then, more gently: “The ink will show you what matters to you; the rest is enforcement you’re not equipped to enjoy.”

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — What the pacts look/feel like**  
> - **Material**  
>   - Visually: old parchment with a faint grain; Lucifer states it is **human skin**.  
>   - Touch: slightly warm, unnervingly soft, but not slimy or bloody.  
> - **Ink behaviour**  
>   - Black ink forms and un‑forms words and pictograms as they look at it.  
>   - Dark red ink highlights certain moving phrases near the margins — failure, disappointment, breach — without ever sitting still long enough to be read clearly.  
> - **Legibility**  
>   - PCs can grasp broad strokes:  
>     - They are agreeing to **retrieve an object** and **deliver it to Lucifer**.  
>     - In return they get **a second life + tailored reward**.  
>   - Any attempt to “lawyer it” runs into **shifting higher‑dimensional logic**; they never catch a hidden “gotcha” clause.  
> - **Golden Ticket link**  
>   - The **front** remains the embossed gold card from A4.  
>   - The **back** is now this living contract; their signature will live here.

> [!warning] **RULE — Pact behaviour (behind the screen)**  
> - **Binding but abstract:**  
>   - Anyone who continues as a PC in the campaign is assumed to have **signed**.  
>   - There is **no separate flag per PC**; don’t track a numeric “pact score.”  
> - **No breaking the pact mid‑campaign:**  
>   - PCs can **betray Lucifer in the climax** (keep the orb, kill him, etc.); that’s handled by story, not by “voiding” the pact now.  
>   - Until then, treat the pact as **background pressure and flavour**, not a mechanical shackle.  
> - **Honest rewards:**  
>   - At the end of the campaign, find a way — however twisted — to **honour what they signed for** (status, power, closure) if they fulfilled their side.  
>   - Lucifer does **not lie** about paying out; the catch is **how** he pays, not *if*.

### Block A5.5 — Signing, Refusing, and Lucifer’s Read

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — The pen on the bar**  
> He produces a slim gold pen and sets it on the bar between you. “Sign on the back of the ticket,” he says, “name and a mark of intent. I do not forge and I do not amend; I only honour what is freely given.”

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — When a PC signs**  
> Pick 1–2 details each time someone signs:  
> - Their name settles onto the contract in **deep black**; a faint echo appears under it in red, like a shadow.  
> - For a heartbeat the view outside the window **flickers** — their old death scene overlaid on the burning city, then gone.  
> - The golden edge of the ticket **flares softly**, then returns to normal; the card feels heavier now.  
> - Lucifer’s expression shifts by a fraction: pleased, intrigued, or faintly bored, depending on what they asked for.

> [!tip] **GM — Table prompts while they sign**  
> - “What does your signature look like — careful, messy, a symbol?”  
> - “When you touch the pen, what flash of your old life do you *almost* remember?”  
> - Make this **quick and punchy**; it’s flavour, not a puzzle.

> [!tip] **GM — If someone refuses to sign**  
> - In fiction, Lucifer stays calm:  
>   - “You’re free to refuse. It just means you stay here while the line runs out.”  
> - Out of character, be clear:  
>   - “If you don’t sign, this character stays in Hell and doesn’t go on the mission. That’s effectively a new‑character / game‑over choice. Are you sure?”  
> - If they still refuse, you can:  
>   - Let that PC become an **NPC** in Hell, and the player rolls a **new soul** who already signed, or  
>   - Have Lucifer gently suggest that “bravery looks better on the other side of the glass” and give them one last chance.

> [!abstract] **GUIDE — Using the psych ledger here**   
> - As they sign and talk, jot down quick tags per PC:  
>   - **Mercy** — spared someone, worried about collateral.  
>   - **Efficiency** — focused on the plan, asked schedule / constraints.  
>   - **Ruthlessness** — eager, dismissive of costs, jokes about Hell.  
>   - **Mark** — how quickly they signed (first, last, reluctant, bargaining).  
> - Use these tags later to **aim Lucifer’s attention and offers**, not as a numeric score.

> [!tip] **GM — Questions about loopholes**  
> - If they ask “What happens if we fail?”:  
>   - “Then the pause ends, the world finishes dying, and you get a front‑row seat. I recommend success.”  
> - If they ask “Can we change the terms?”:  
>   - Lightly: “You are negotiating from a position of zero leverage. Fortunately, I’m already being generous.”  
> - Remember: he **never lies**, but he’s happy to remind them the alternative is **staying in Hell**.

### Block A5.6 — Bridge, Mirror, and Exit to Revival

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Door to the bridge**  
> Once the last signature dries, the whole car gives a slow, satisfied shiver. Lucifer straightens his coat, nods toward the rear door, and with a lazy gesture it swings open onto a narrow platform hanging over an impossible bridge of stone and steel.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Mirror on the span**  
> A short set of steps leads up onto the bridge proper, stretching out into a crimson haze in both directions while the train idles beside it. A human‑height mirror waits a few paces away, its surface showing not your reflections but a stark, fluorescent‑lit room on the other side.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Lucifer’s final push**  
> “This brings us as close as I can get you,” he says quietly. “Step through, and you wake up somewhere that still remembers your face; stay here, and you keep me company until the bridge runs out.”

> [!tip] **GM — How to handle the crossing**  
> - Give them a moment to react, talk, or hesitate on the bridge.  
> - Stepping through the mirror is the **hard cut** to Revival / character creation.  
> - If someone balks, Lucifer does **not** shove them; the **choice must be voluntary**. Remind them out of character what refusal means.

> [!success] **OUTCOME — When Scene A5 is done**  
> - All continuing PCs have **signed Lucifer’s pact** on the backs of their Golden Tickets.  
> - Golden Tickets now:  
>   - Still exist as items, with **name + signature** on the living contract side.  
>   - Remain effectively **indestructible in practice** as in A4: if “lost,” they reappear on the PC between scenes.   
> - PCs have stepped (or are about to step) through the **mirror gate** toward the Revival room.  
> - System is **about to turn ON**: HP, skills, Injuries, CDR, currencies, etc. all come online in the **Revival** procedure; **Chaos & Harmony** still stay dormant until the later temple sequence.   
> - Lucifer remains behind in Hell, preparing his ritual and waiting at the **end of the world** for the Orb.  
>
