

> [!summary] **SCENE A4 — Gossip Car & Golden Tickets**  
> TYPE: Talk, Explore, Downtime  
> LENGTH: Medium  
> DANGER: Safe, Homebase  
> PURPOSE: Setup  
> TEACHING: Light  
>  
> **When this runs:** Immediately after Scene A3, once all PCs are seated in the sealed carriage and the train has started moving but **before** they reach the bar‑car / Sundown Saloon.  
>  
> **Story job:**  
> - Give the group a **breather** and a clear place to talk about what’s happening.  
> - Show that the Train has **rules and expectations** (pamphlet, signage, etiquette).  
> - Deliver unsettling desk details: **signature‑practice sheet**, **torn diary pages**, **empty ashtray**.  
> - Reveal that each PC already has a **Golden Ticket** in their pocket.  
> - Reuse the Android as a **Ticket Inspector** who checks tickets and then escorts them to Lucifer.  
>  
> **Player job:**  
> - Share theories about where they are and who’s running this.  
> - Engage with props in the car (desk, pamphlets, diary scraps, signature sheet, luggage).  
> - Discover the **Golden Ticket** pamphlet and then their own tickets — or be surprised when the Inspector prompts them.  
>  
> **Tone:**  
> - Quiet, uncanny “waiting room”; almost banal if you squint.  
> - Darkly humorous: Hell has a **train etiquette pamphlet**, a ticket inspector, and someone practising signatures.

> [!danger] **RULE — System still OFF (pre‑Revival)**  
> - Same as A1–A3:  
>   - No HP, damage, skills, saves, spells, Chaos, CDR, Harmony, currencies, or Injuries.  
>   - No rolls that matter; any dice are **pure flavour** if you use them.  
> - This is a pure **talking and looking** scene; nothing here can hurt or mechanically change the PCs.

### Block A4.1 — Arrival in the Gossip Car

> [!question] **TRIGGER — When to run this scene**  
> - After A3’s carriage departure description, the train briefly “jumps” and then:  
>   - The lights flicker,  
>   - The door at the far end opens,  
>   - The Android gestures them into the **next car**.  
> - Use this scene before you move to the actual bar‑car / Sundown Saloon with Lucifer.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Entering the Gossip Car**  
> The humming carriage steadies, then gives a little sideways shudder.  
>  
> At the far end, a door slides open with a smooth sigh. The uniformed figure from the platform stands there, perfectly still.  
>  
> “Please change carriages,” he says. “This way.”  
>  
> He leads you through into a smaller, brighter compartment — a low‑ceilinged lounge with padded benches, a narrow table under the window, a cluttered little writing desk, and a few pieces of luggage and loose papers abandoned in tidy chaos.

> [!abstract] **FEATURES — The Gossip Car at a glance**  
> - **Nameplate / Signage**  
>   - A polished plaque over the door reads:  
>     - “PASSENGER ACCLIMATION LOUNGE” in formal script;  
>     - Someone has lightly scratched “GOSSIP CAR” into one corner.  
> - **Seating**  
>   - Benches along both sides facing each other, clearly meant for conversation.  
>   - Enough space for the party to sit together.  
> - **Window**  
>   - One long window showing streaks of red rail and dark outside; not the focus, just a reminder the train is moving.  
> - **Desk corner**  
>   - A small writing desk bolted to the wall with:  
>     - A page **full of signatures**,  
>     - Several **torn diary pages**,  
>     - A **Passenger Conduct Pamphlet**,  
>     - An **empty glass ashtray**.  
> - **Luggage**  
>   - A small satchel or briefcase tucked under the bench near the desk, with a worn luggage tag.  
> - **Ambient detail**  
>   - Low, warm lighting; faint hum and vibration; no other passengers in sight.

> [!tip] **GM — What the Android does here**  
> - He steps aside and gestures at the seats:  
>   - “Please be seated. The inspector will attend to you shortly.”  
> - Then he exits through the far door and is **gone** for a while.  
> - No further interaction until you bring him back as Ticket Inspector (A4.4).

### Block A4.2 — Desk Papers, Diary Scraps, and Ashtray

> [!tip] **GM — Use these to seed conversation**  
> - Let players discover these organically.  
> - When there’s a lull, draw someone’s attention to one of these items.

#### Signature‑practice sheet

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Page of signatures**  
> - A single sheet torn from a notebook or ledger, covered edge‑to‑edge with **signatures**.  
> - Same hand, same name, over and over — but the name itself is hard to make out; letters blur if you stare.  
> - Some are neat, some angry, some almost scratched through the paper.  
> - Optional creepy detail: one or two signatures **almost** look like a PC’s name or initial arrangement, before your eyes slide off.  
> - Suggested prompt:  
>   - “Whoever sat here spent a long time practising one signature over and over. What does that make you think you might be asked to sign?”

#### Torn diary pages

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Diary scraps**  
> - 2–3 torn paperback‑sized pages, edges ragged, written in a different, more intimate hand.  
> - Fragments like:  
>   - “…they told me it would be a simple transfer, just sign and ride, but the way the inspector looked at me…”  
>   - “…if this ends up in the wrong hands, maybe you’ll think twice before you sign anything they hand you…”  
>   - “…the world was already ending when I stepped onto the platform. This train just made it official…”  
> - Nothing directly explains the cosmology; it’s all **last impressions**, fear, resignation, dark humour.  
> - Suggested prompt:  
>   - “Reading this, whose voice do you imagine — someone like you, or someone very different?”

#### Empty ashtray

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Ashtray**  
> - A heavy glass ashtray sits on the desk, **perfectly empty**, but:  
>   - There’s a faint smell of smoke baked into it.  
>   - If someone tips it or sniffs closely, a smear of grey dust clings to the glass.  
> - You can use it as a throwaway line about “someone sat here long enough to smoke through their nerves,” or leave it as quiet evidence of a previous occupant.

#### Left luggage

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Satchel / briefcase**  
> - Small case with an old‑style **luggage tag**:  
>   - “FROM: [SMUDGED]”  
>   - “TO: TERMINUS — RED LINE”  
> - Inside, if opened:  
>   - Mundane items from a prior life (pick 1–2):  
>     - A worker’s jacket and a pay stub from a factory or office.  
>     - A folded photo of a station or city skyline, faces blurred.  
>     - A notebook with a few blank pages and one line: “If you’re reading this, I didn’t make it past the bar.”  
> - Purpose: reinforce that **others** came through this process.

### Block A4.3 — Behaviour Pamphlet & Golden Ticket Discovery

> [!tip] **GM — This is the key “procedure” prop**  
> - The pamphlet tells them what’s “supposed” to happen and hints strongly that they already have tickets.  
> - Ideally, a PC reads it and then **chooses** to check their pockets.

#### Passenger Conduct Pamphlet

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — “Passengers, Please” pamphlet**  
> - A folded card in neat stacks on the desk.  
> - Front: **APOCALYPSE EXPRESS — PASSENGER COURTESY NOTICE** with a Red Line crest.  
> - Inside, simple pictograms and short captions:  
>   1. **Sit** — soul‑shape sitting on a bench.  
>   2. **Speak Softly** — two silhouettes with tiny speech bubbles.  
>   3. **Prepare** — soul with a hand in its pocket, a small ticket icon by its side.  
>   4. **Present & Sign** — soul handing over a ticket to a conductor figure, then signing on a glowing line.  
> - Text snippets:  
>   - “Please remain seated in the Courtesy Car.”  
>   - “Quiet conversation is encouraged.”  
>   - “The Ticket Inspector will arrive shortly.”  
>   - “When prompted, present the **golden ticket from your pocket** and sign where indicated.”

> [!tip] **GM — Prompt to check pockets**  
> - When a player reads that line out loud or paraphrases it, say:  
>   - “As you hit the words ‘golden ticket from your pocket,’ you all become suddenly aware of a small, stiff rectangle sitting in or against you somewhere.”  
> - If no one reads the pamphlet, the Inspector in A4.4 will force the reveal later.

#### Golden Tickets (ideal case: player‑driven discovery)

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Tickets in every pocket**  
> - Each PC finds, in whatever counts as a pocket or inner space:  
>   - A **stiff gold card** about the size of a train ticket.  
>   - Front: embossed Red Line sigil (two rails cutting through a circle).  
>   - Back:  
>     - A blank line for a **name** (currently empty).  
>     - A dotted line labelled “SIGNATURE / MARK” at the bottom.  
> - They feel more **solid and real** than the PCs’ current forms.  
> - If tossed aside, burnt, or torn:  
>   - It doesn’t resist, but the next time they check, an identical ticket is back in their pocket.

> [!warning] **RULE — Ticket behaviour (behind the screen)**  
> - In this scene, tickets are **indestructible in practice**:  
>   - Any attempt to get rid of one quietly resets it to “in the pocket.”  
> - Note `golden_tickets_discovered = true` once at least one PC finds theirs.  
> - Do **not** have them sign yet; the signature line is for the pact with Lucifer, not this car.

### Block A4.4 — Talk Time & Table Prompts

> [!tip] **GM — Let them talk**  
> - Give them space to process, argue, joke, and theorise.  
> - Use light prompts if they stall.

> [!abstract] **PROMPTS — If conversation stalls**  
> - “Looking at that page of signatures, what do you think someone was practising so hard to sign?”  
> - “Those diary scraps suggest someone regretted signing something. Does that change how you feel about your own ticket?”  
> - “The pamphlet makes this sound routine. Does that make it better or worse?”  
> - “Does your soul feel more like a passenger, a prisoner, or staff that got demoted?”  
> - You can also let the car react subtly:  
>   - A little shiver in the floor when someone says “Devil.”  
>   - A soft chime when someone suggests “we could just refuse.”

> [!warning] **RULE — Still no mechanics explained**  
> - Avoid explaining Chaos, CDR, Revival, etc., here.  
> - In‑world answers to “what happens if we die again?” are still vague:  
>   - “That’s not my department,” if they imagine staff.  
>   - Or “You’ll find out soon enough,” if Lucifer comes up.

### Block A4.5 — Ticket Inspection & Escort

> [!tip] **GM — When to bring the Android back**  
> - Once:  
>   - They’ve had a satisfying amount of talk, **and**  
>   - Golden tickets have been discovered (either via pamphlet or you’re ready to reveal them),  
> - Then reintroduce the Android as Inspector.

> [!quote] **READ ALOUD — Inspector returns**  
> The door at the far end slides open again.  
>  
> The same uniformed figure steps in, now wearing a narrow armband stamped with the Red Line crest and carrying a slim metal clipboard.  
>  
> “Ticket inspection,” he says. “Please remain seated. Present your Golden Tickets.”

#### If they already know about the tickets

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Smooth inspection**  
> - PCs who have already found their tickets can produce them immediately.  
> - The Android:  
>   - Holds each ticket for a second, taps the clipboard once per soul, then hands it back.  
>   - Never questions legitimacy; he is only checking existence.

#### If they don’t know yet

> [!abstract] **FEATURE — Forced discovery**  
> - PCs look confused / deny having tickets.  
> - The Android answers:  
>   - “Your Golden Ticket will be in your pocket. Please check now.”  
>   - If they still hesitate: “There has never been an instance of a manifested soul without a ticket.”  
> - When they check, they find the ticket as described in A4.3.  
> - Inspection then proceeds as above.

> [!warning] **RULE — Tickets still non‑mechanical**  
> - Do not take the tickets away permanently.  
> - Do not have them sign yet.  
> - Note `golden_tickets_validated = true` once every PC has shown or discovered theirs.

> [!tip] **GM — Escort to Sundown Saloon**  
> - After the last ticket is checked, the Android steps back, gestures toward the far door:  
>   - “Thank you. Please proceed to the Sundown Saloon for final processing.”  
> - He opens the internal door and leads them forward through one or two connecting cars.  
>   - You may describe glimpses of dark or freight cars as they pass, but all doors remain closed to them.

> [!success] **OUTCOME — Flags & next scene**  
> - Mark: `gossip_car_run = true`.  
> - Mark: `golden_tickets_discovered = true`.  
> - Mark: `golden_tickets_validated = true`.  
> - PCs should understand:  
>   - They are **expected passengers** with pre‑issued tickets, not random strays.  
>   - This train runs on **procedures and paperwork**, not chaos alone.  
>   - They are being moved toward a more serious “signing” interaction.  
>
