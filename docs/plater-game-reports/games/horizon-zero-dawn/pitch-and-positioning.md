# Horizon Zero Dawn — Pitch and Positioning Draft

Status: **Pre-run positioning; not a verdict**

## One-line pitch

**A Mass Effect-like lost-civilization mystery inside a Witcher-lite open world, without Dragon Age's party, romance, or branching-roleplay depth, but with tactical machine hunting and no co-op dependency.**

## What it appears to be

- A third-person, solo, open-world action RPG built around exploration, ranged hunting, stealth, traps, crafting, and machine weak-point analysis.
- A defined-protagonist story about Aloy and the relationship between a tribal present, machine ecology, and the lost old world.
- A mystery-led experience where discovering why the world exists in this form may matter more than changing its plot through choices.
- An adjustable-friction first run whose combat can be approached as preparation and problem-solving rather than Soulslike endurance.

## What it is not

- Not a party-based Dragon Age or Mass Effect replacement.
- Not a romance or companion-approval game.
- Not a deeply branching narrative RPG.
- Not a Soulslike and not built around mandatory boss retry walls.
- Not co-op and not dependent on a partner for story or platform completion.
- Not automatically worth clearing to 79/79; mastery must earn that commitment after credits.

## Comparison map

### The Witcher 3

Shared promise: large world, tracking/hunting preparation, quests, strong setting.
Expected difference: lighter dialogue choice, moral consequence, side-quest writing, and roleplay depth; more tactical ranged hunting.

### Mass Effect

Shared promise: archaeological mystery, old-world revelations, large-scale lore questions.
Expected difference: no squad management, loyalty structure, romance framework, or Commander Shepard-style role authorship.

### Dragon Age

Shared promise: lore, factions, heroic journey, and a world worth inhabiting.
Expected difference: no persistent party, approval system, tactical-pause identity, or broad player-defined protagonist.

### Days Gone

Shared promise: open-world traversal, camps, crafting, territorial threats, and post-collapse discovery.
Expected difference: machine ecology rather than hordes, less road-trip survival identity, and more lost-civilization mystery.

### The Lord of the Rings: War in the North

Expected relief: no co-op requirement, less corridor-brawler structure, and difficulty that can be adjusted when an encounter stops being enjoyable.
Remaining risk: multiple machines can still create chaotic pressure, so swarm frustration must be logged rather than dismissed.

## Review thesis candidate

**Can an exceptional mystery and a satisfying machine-hunting grammar make a comparatively thin roleplaying layer feel focused rather than missing?**
