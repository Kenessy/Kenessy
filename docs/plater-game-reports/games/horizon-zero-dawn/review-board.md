## Pre-run hypotheses

### H1 — Mystery carrier

The lost-world mystery will be the main narrative engine, closer to the archaeological pull of Mass Effect than to Dragon Age's party politics.

- Evidence for: the premise prompted an immediate world-structure question, followed by conditional interest in seeing more cave-to-old-world environments.
- Evidence against: none yet.
- Falsifier: the first major reveals feel obvious, detached, or delivered without player curiosity.

### H2 — Tactical but non-Soulslike combat

Scanning, elemental tools, traps, stealth, and component targeting will make difficulty feel solvable rather than punitive.

- Evidence for: game-system preflight only; no Daniel gameplay evidence.
- Evidence against: Daniel specifically dislikes swarm-heavy difficulty spikes.
- Falsifier: repeated encounters collapse into unavoidable mob pressure or long retry walls even after preparation and setting adjustment.

### H3 — Roleplay gap

The game may satisfy world and story hunger while underserving the companion, romance, and branching-choice expectations created by Dragon Age and Mass Effect.

- Evidence for: positioning analysis only.
- Evidence against: a strong defined protagonist can still create attachment and authored agency.
- Falsifier: Daniel experiences the fixed protagonist as more compelling than a broader but thinner choice system.

### H4 — Checklist tolerance

Optional map activity will be enjoyable while it teaches machine ecology or world history, but harmful when pursued only for icons or achievements.

- Falsifier: either cleanup stays intrinsically fun, or even the supposedly meaningful side content feels like filler.
