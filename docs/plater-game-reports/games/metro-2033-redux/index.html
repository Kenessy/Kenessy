Reviewer Note

Short human verdict

Metro does not rely on any single ingredient being exceptional on its own. It works because the final stew has a bitter, smoky, nostalgic taste that none of those ingredients could create alone. Small lights, desperate promises, and half-heard station stories push Artyom forward until the journey becomes the kind of impossible tale survivors would trade around a drafty station fire: too chilling to dismiss, too strange to fully believe.

The world shifts around Artyom as he moves through it: warmly lit inhabited stations, half-abandoned service tunnels with a stray bulb still buzzing, sudden muzzle flashes cutting through the dark, and the bright surface with its poisonous air and irradiated ruins. Every place sets its own terms of survival. Artyom adapts to those terms, or the Metro swallows him.

What stays with you is not a handful of shiny pre-war bullets, a few mutant fights, or a stealth route past armed guards. It is all of them together, and the story they coherently tell: a tale about human fear, the unknown, and the ascent from the deepest dark of the Metro, where Artyom’s survival depends on the mercy of others, to the roof of the world, where he finally looks down, casts light on what lived below, and holds not only his own fate, but the fate of others in his hands.
