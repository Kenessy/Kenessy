Page 01 - Act 1

The University Before Time Breaks

The first proper journey page: Riverport University, Paul Serene, Project Promenade, and the last quiet walk before the time machine.

Image-ready comic page

Project Promenade

4 image slots16:9 first passSpoiler level: low / setup premise
Read-aloud story beat

The last normal walk

Jack Joyce arrives at Riverport University before dawn, pulled in by Paul Serene, an old friend who is clearly hiding the scale of what he is about to reveal. Outside, protestors are already pushing back against Monarch and the university's demolition plans.

Inside, the building is quiet, glossy, and overcontrolled: blue-lit doors, glassy lab surfaces, polished corridors, and the sterile confidence of a project that has gone too far to be stopped politely.

Paul leads Jack deeper into the facility while explaining just enough to keep the mystery moving. It feels authored, dense, and deliberate: not an open playground, but a guided walk into a machine that has already started bending the story around itself.

What this proves

Authored momentum

The opening supports the Remedy cinematic-experiment lens: constant dialogue, strong environmental readability, and controlled sci-fi mood.

Friction note

Exposition density

The setup is heavy, but it currently helps because the scene is a guided lab tour. The review must watch whether the game later overexplains.

Review impact

Early positive delta

Narrative Pull +1. Atmosphere +1. Readability +1. Technical Readiness remains unscored until movement, video playback, combat, and frame pacing are checked.

Page 02 - Act 1

The Machine Breaks

The experiment stops being a pitch and becomes the first real fracture: two-minute proof, five-minute escalation, unclear detonation, frozen-time rescue, and the first combat power.

Replay note converted to comic page

First Stutter

4 image slots16:9 first passSpoiler level: early premise
Read-aloud story beat

Time moves, then stops

The demonstration appears to prove the machine works: Paul sends the object back two minutes, then immediately pushes the test into a five-minute forward jump. Will breaks in with the warning that time is going to end, but the useful explanation arrives late.

The core detonates while everyone is still inside the experiment. The important read from the replay note is causality fog: it does not look like someone simply shoots the core; it feels like the machine breaks itself while the characters are still arguing with half-sentences.

The strongest moment is the first frozen-time state. The room hangs in a broken instant, Will is trapped in the stutter, and Jack wakes him by touching him. Enemies fire, Jack slows or stops the danger, and the first weapon arrives without the page needing a long tutorial.

The messy part is communication. Will keeps repeating the warning, but the scene withholds the practical reason long enough that the player note becomes: time is an egg, and the egg is broken. More precisely: there is a fracture in time, and it is breaking down.

What this proves

Spectacle becomes play

The first stutter works because it converts story spectacle into interaction: frozen people, readable danger, touch-to-unfreeze logic, and a clean first power/weapon handoff.

Friction note

Communication fog

Will's warning has urgency, but the scene leans on withheld explanation and repeated half-warnings. The review should watch whether mystery stays exciting or becomes avoidable confusion.

Review impact

Positive, caveated

Atmosphere +1, Engagement +1, Readability watch. Combat is promising after the first time-slow beat, but it is still too early to score beyond the intro handoff.
